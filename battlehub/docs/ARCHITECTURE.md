# The architecture engine

BattleHub's lobby, the **Citadel**, is generated by a procedural architecture engine in
`net.battlehub.arch`. The engine designs buildings the way an architect would: it writes a brief, plans the
masses, decomposes them into assemblies and reusable modules, gives every block a role, and only then decides
materials through shared palettes and gradients. It checks the result before anything is placed. This document
covers the deliverables of the engine, A to J.

## A. What the project had, what was missing, and the constraints

**Already there:** a blueprint system for arenas (`arena/Blueprint`, `Build.s` for block names with properties,
`ArenaBuilder`, which places blocks over several ticks). A drawing kit for the old hub (`HubKit`) with gradients,
discs, cones and trees. Floor patterns and value noise (`core/Noise`, `core/FloorPattern`), A* walking routes
(`core/GridPath`), and an isometric preview for the self-test log (`debug/Preview`).

**Missing:** any notion of architecture. Buildings were hand-coded block by block, with no reusable modules,
roles, palettes per role, building-wide gradients, roofs that join, validation, or way to see a design without
starting a server.

**Constraints that shape the design:**
* BattleHub runs **on the server only**, so vanilla clients can join. It cannot ship new blocks. The "custom
  blocks" are therefore Minecraft 26.3's own blocks, plus block families from other mods that the engine finds
  in the registry at start-up (`lobby.discoverModBlocks`).
* Blocks are placed **without neighbour updates** (fast and safe for big builds). Shapes the game would normally
  work out itself are computed by the engine: wall, fence and pane connections, and stair corners.
* There is **no Minecraft client in CI**, so there are no real screenshots. The engine has its own ray tracer,
  and its pictures go into the CI log as base64 JPEG.
* The engine is **pure Java** (no Minecraft imports). It runs and is tested without the game. A small bridge in
  `hub/ArchLobby` turns its output into a blueprint.

## B. Architecture

```
Dimension palette ─┬─ District palettes ── Building palette (style recipe)
                   │
Lobby (settlement) ─ terrain · plaza · avenues · streets · plots · landmarks · scenery
   │
Architect.build(brief) ─ plan (volumes) → Massing.check → assemble (modules) → Weathering → materials
   │                                            │
   │                                            └─ Facade (bays) · Towers · Assembler (foundations,
   │                                               floors, roofs, interiors) · Fort · RoofField
   ▼
Building (plan in building coordinates, frame into the world, attachments, focal points)
   │  emit: Resolver (role → ramp → gradient → coherent pick → form → weathering → name)
   ▼
Voxels (finished block names) ─ Connect (walls, panes, stairs) ─ Validator ─ Render / Blueprint
```

| Package | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| `arch` | Core types: `Dir`, `Xform` (frames: turns, mirror, offset, composition), `Cell` (role, form, direction, half, axis, priority, flags, variant, literal; interned), `Plan` (sparse cells with priorities), `Voxels` (finished names), `Rng` (named seeded streams), `Noise3`, `Easing`, `Props`. |
| `arch.mat` | `Catalog` (families with their 26.3 forms, average colours, tags, aged variants, stand-ins; kinds of single blocks), `Material`, `Ramp`, `Palette` (inheritance), `Gradient` (+ `Stack`), `MaterialContext`, `Resolver`, `Connect`. |
| `arch.geom` | `ArchCurve` (round, segmental, pointed, lancet, four-centred, flat), `Footprint` (rectangle, octagon, circle, clip, union). |
| `arch.mod` | The module contract (`Module`, `Spec`, `Attachment`, `Category`, `Ctx`), the `Library`, the module families and `RoofField`. |
| `arch.bldg` | `Style`, `Brief`, `Volume`, `Condition`, `Randomness`, `Building`, `Architect`, `Massing`, `Facade`, `Assembler`, `Towers`, `Fort`, `Weathering` and the archetypes. |
| `arch.town` | The lobby: `Terrain`, `Regions` (palettes), `Plaza` (authored module), `Occupancy`, `Lobby`. |
| `arch.check` | `Validator`, `Report`, `Walk` (walkability for `GridPath`). |
| `arch.render` | `Render`, the ray tracer. |

**Generation stages.** Every stage has its own random stream (`Rng.stream("massing")`, `"assembly"`,
`"decor:…"`, `"weathering"`, `"materials"`). Changing decoration therefore never moves a wall. The same seed
always gives the same building.

1. **Brief** (`Archetype.brief`): archetype, style, sizes, storeys, the archetype's choices (towers, transept,
   crown...), palette recipe, condition, ornament, detail. This is the shared source of truth.
2. **Massing** (`Archetype.plan`): volumes, each with a role (nave, aisle, tower, keep...), a footprint, a floor
   and an eave height, a roof kind, a wall thickness, storeys, a rank (0 dominant, 1 secondary, 2 accent) and
   a front. `Massing.check` makes sure the dominant mass stands highest.
3. **Assembly** (`Archetype.assemble`): foundations down to the ground, then walls. Straight runs go through the
   facade composer and curved shells through `Towers`. Then floors, interior space, all roofs in one height
   field, interiors and ornaments.
4. **Condition** (`Weathering`): roofs open in patches, crowns crumble from the top, columns break and their drums
   lie beside them, breaches open (decoration before structure), and upper floors fall in. Then everything that
   no longer connects to the ground falls. Rubble lies at the foot of the walls and plants take over. Paths
   marked `KEEP` stay clear.
5. **Materials** (`Building.materials`): the shared context. See E.
6. **Validation** (`Validator`): see F.

**Coordinates.** Modules draw in their own frame: x along the front, y up, and z outwards (z = 0 is the wall's
outer face). Buildings draw in building coordinates, with the main entrance facing local +z. `Xform` places
module frames in the building and the building in the world, turning stairs, doors, logs and literal block
properties along with them.

**Priorities.** A cell replaces another only with an equal or higher priority (`FILL < BODY < DETAIL < OPEN <
FEATURE`). A window cut into a wall therefore stays open when more wall is drawn over it. Decoration can overlap
a wall, but a wall never fills an opening.

## C. Implementation (the working vertical slice and beyond)

The first slice was a fortified building (the castle). It has a foundation, wall runs, several window kinds, a
gate, towers, roofs, ornaments, a shared palette, a building-wide gradient, valid connections, deterministic
output and validation. It runs through the same code path every other building uses. On top of that slice the
engine now has eight archetypes:

| Archetype | Grammar (simplified) | Varies |
|---|---|---|
| `cathedral` | West front + nave + aisles? + transept? + choir + apse + roofs + buttresses + interior. The front is a portal, a rose window, a gable and twin towers, one tower, or a screen with turrets. Nave bays are an arcade arch, a clerestory window and vault ribs. | bays, nave width, aisles, front, spires, transept, crossing (flèche, lantern, none), apse (polygonal or flat), palette |
| `castle` | Curtain wall with wall walk + corner towers (+ towers along long walls) + gatehouse + keep + great hall + courtyard (stairs to the walks, a well, trees) | plan (rectangle or octagon), size, tower shape, crown (battlements, machicolations, cones), keep (square with turrets or round), keep side, wall height |
| `mage_tower` | Terrace + stages (each narrower, with a balcony and a rune band) + crown + turrets at different heights + annex + floating crystals and rune ring. One spiral stair runs through every stage. | radius, stages, plan (round or octagon), crown (crystal, spire, dome), turrets, annex |
| `temple_ruin` | Precinct with stoa and gateway + podium with steps + peripteros (colonnade, cella, entablature, pediment) or tholos (ring of columns, dome) + obelisks. Built whole, then aged. | columns, proportions, temple kind, precinct size, obelisks, condition (ruined or ancient) |
| `townhouse` | Stone ground floor + timbered or brick upper storeys + gable (to the street or along it) or hipped roof + dormer + chimney + furnished room. The shop variant has an awning. | size, storeys, roof, shop, dormers, district palette |
| `watchtower` | Battered tower with slits, door and crown (+ turrets when wide) | shape, height, crown |
| `chapel` | Nave with buttressed lancet bays + bell tower (front or side) with an open belfry and spire + apse + pews | width, length, tower position, apse |
| `market_hall` | Open arcade + timbered upper hall + hipped roof + bell lantern + market stalls | size, palette |

## D. The module library

Modules are registered in `Library.defaults()`. The assembly code finds them by category, style and size, so
a new module needs no change anywhere else (see `LibraryTest.aNewModuleIsUsedWithoutTouchingTheEngine`).

| Category | Modules |
|---|---|
| Walls | `wall.body` (masonry with plinth and water table, string courses, cornice, long-and-short quoins), `wall.timber` (half-timbering with braces), `wall.parapet` (battlements, optional machicolations on corbels), `wall.arcade` |
| Windows | `window.rect` (lintel, sill, shutters, window boxes), `window.slit` (splayed, optional cross slit), `window.lancet` (frame, hood moulding, composed stained glass), `window.twin` (two lancets, mullion, oculus: tracery), `window.rose` (radial tracery, colour rings, alternating petals; a focal point), `window.oculus`, `window.round` (arch ring, keystone), `window.arcane`, `window.blind` (blind arcade), `window.niche`, `window.oriel` (corbelled bay) |
| Doors | `door.plain`, `door.double` (arch, transom), `door.portal` (porch with stepped archivolts, tympanum, trumeau, wimperg, pinnacles; a focal point), `door.gate` (portcullis in its groove), `door.temple` (lintel, pediment), `door.arcane` |
| Piers | `pier.pilaster`, `pier.buttress` (set-backs with sloping tops, pinnacles above the eaves), `pier.column`, `pier.none` |
| Roofs | `roof.gable`, `roof.hip`, `roof.cone`, `roof.spire`, `roof.dome`, `roof.flat` (parapet or battlements). Inside buildings, roofs are drawn together through `RoofField`: gable, hip, shed, cone, octagonal spire and dome shapes, with gable end walls filled and towers masked out. |
| Towers | `Towers.build` handles round, octagonal and square shafts: battered base, string courses, openings that turn from storey to storey, floors, a spiral stair (with landings), the door, a balcony, and crowns (battlements, machicolations, cone, spire, dome, belfry with a bell, crystal cage) with corbelled turrets. |
| Ornaments | `orn.finial`, `orn.gargoyle`, `orn.banner`, `orn.lantern` (on chains), `orn.sconce`, `orn.chimney` (smoking), `orn.dormer`, `orn.crystal` (meant to float), `orn.urn`, `orn.planter`, `orn.tree`, `orn.lamppost` |
| Interiors | `int.nave` (pews, carpet, altar platform, candles, chandeliers), `int.hall` (long table, throne, banners), `int.library` (shelves only against solid wall), `int.house`, `int.arcane` (enchanting table in a ring of shelves), `int.shrine` |
| Fortification | `Fort.curtain` (any plan: battered foot, wall walk, merlons, string course, slits), `Fort.stairRun`, `Fort.walkDoors`, `Fort.well` |

The facade composer (`Facade`) lays a run out symmetrically from the middle (corners, piers and bays). It uses
one window type per storey for the whole run, so repetition reads as rhythm. The entrance goes in the central bay
of the main front. Variation is structured: glass schemes alternate from the middle out, and shutters and window
boxes come from the decoration stream. The bay grid never changes.

**Authored modules** are supported next to procedural ones. The plaza (`town/Plaza`) is hand-designed with exact
blocks and placed by the same lobby assembly.

## E. Materials and gradients

* **Catalog** (`mat/Catalog`): every family with the forms it really has in 26.3 (stairs, slab, wall, fence,
  pane, pillar, trapdoor, door), its average texture colour, semantic tags (stone, brick, dark, light, marble,
  magic, metal, copper...), aged variants (mossy, cracked, oxidised) and a stand-in for missing forms. Families
  the game lacks are dropped at start-up and missing forms are removed (`hub/ArchMaterials`). Families from other
  mods can be discovered, with the game's map colour and tags from their name.
* **Palettes** (`mat/Palette`): ramps per role, inherited from dimension to district to building. A building
  palette is one of its style's recipes (gothic: verdigris, limestone, obsidian; fortress: granite, sandstone,
  dark keep, brick; arcane: amethyst, end stone, tidal; ancient: desert, marble, overgrown; town: timber, brick,
  stone). Roofs, paving and timber come from the district, so neighbouring houses share a family likeness.
* **Ramps** (`mat/Ramp`): ordered materials (dark to light), each with a share (a large share gives a dominant
  material) and a border width where neighbours interlock.
* **Gradient fields** (`mat/Gradient`): vertical, horizontal, edges, radial, world-radial, noise and constant
  fields, combined in a `Stack`. The stack computes the normalised blend `G(p) = Σ wᵢ gᵢ(p) / Σ wᵢ`, adds local
  influences as offsets (lighter near focal points such as portals and rose windows, darker towards the outer
  edges), adds low-amplitude coherent noise `t' = clamp(G + α N(p))`, and applies easing (linear, smoothstep,
  smootherstep, quadratic, cubic). Everything is evaluated in **building coordinates**, so a gradient never
  restarts at a module seam (`MaterialTest.gradientsAreContinuousAcrossModuleBoundaries`). The vertical wall
  gradient spans the range where the walls actually are (up to the 85th percentile of wall heights). A
  cathedral's nave is therefore not squeezed into the bottom by its spires, and a slender tower gets the whole
  run.
* **Resolution** (`mat/Resolver`) works in layers:
  1. role and form;
  2. the role's ramp;
  3. the gradient value at the building position;
  4. the ramp entry, picked with a coherent per-stone random number (two-block stones in running bond, mixed
     with patch noise), so borders form patches, not speckle (`MaterialTest.stoneByStoneMixingFormsPatchesNotNoise`).
     Frames, courses and edges change material at a clear line instead;
  5. a form the material really has: the nearest ramp entry with it, then the family's stand-in, then the
     family closest in colour that has it;
  6. weathering, where it shows (moss low down, cracks higher up, in patches);
  7. the name with its properties.

  Glass stays glass and roofs only get roof materials (`BuildingTest.rolesKeepTheirMaterials`).
* **Stained glass** is composed, not random. Modules pick colour positions in the palette's ordered glass ramp
  (base, band and head of a lancet; rings and alternating petals of a rose).

## F. Validation and tests

`Validator.building` checks:
* geometry (size budget, directions, unknown decoration names);
* structure: blocks not connected to the ground through faces or edges, unless they are meant to float;
* entrances: clear space for a player at each `ENTRANCE` attachment;
* stairs: a `GridPath` walk from every stair's bottom landing to its top;
* roofs: no wall top of a roofed volume left open to the sky;
* palettes: no jump of more than 32% brightness between neighbouring ramp entries;
* composition metrics: height variation, skyline steps and height.

`Validator.lobby` adds known block names, no overlapping buildings, and a walkable way from the spawn to the
entrance of every landmark. The lobby is validated every time it is generated, and the report goes to the server
log.

JUnit (`src/test/java/net/battlehub/arch`, run without Minecraft) covers the 16 required checks:
1. reproducibility (`sameSeedSameBuilding`);
2. variation across seeds (`differentSeedsGiveDifferentDesigns`, `anotherSeedIsAnotherCity`);
3. frames and directions (`TransformTest`);
4. attachments after transformation;
5. gradient continuity across module seams;
6. roles kept;
7. missing blocks falling back safely;
8. required components;
9. stairs connecting their levels;
10. roofs covering their walls;
11. lobby bounds;
12. palettes climbing without jumps;
13. a failing archetype writes nothing;
14. decoration can be left out without touching the structure;
15. more detail keeps the massing;
16. a new module is used without engine changes.

Further tests cover every archetype passing its checks over six seeds, every catalog block existing in the 26.3
block list, roofs crossing without holes, arch profiles, plan priorities and the whole lobby.

The **self-test in a real 26.3 server** (`debug/ArchTests`) checks that:
* the lobby came from the engine and passed validation;
* every catalog block exists in the game;
* the placed blocks match the plan (sampled);
* a player can walk from the spawn to every landmark through the real world (collision shapes);
* example buildings of every archetype pass their checks.

It also logs ray-traced pictures of the lobby (an overview, a view from the south-east and the view from the
spawn) and of example buildings.

**Visual verification.** `render/Render` traces rays through a voxel grid. It uses the real block shapes (stairs,
slabs, walls, fences, panes, doors, trapdoors, carpets, small decorations), sunlight with shadows, ambient
occlusion, see-through glass and water, glowing blocks, a sky and haze. Every archetype and the lobby were
rendered and inspected while the engine was built, and many rules came from what the pictures showed:
* the gradient follows where the walls really are;
* local influences are offsets, not blend members;
* material patches are larger and frames calmer;
* trims are mid-tones;
* a crossing tower rises above the ridge;
* the rose window may rise into the gable;
* foundations are only for towers that stand on the ground;
* roof steps rest on each other;
* bushes stay out of gateways.

## G. Example configurations

The four example profiles go through the same architecture:

```java
Library lib = Library.defaults();
Palette dim = net.battlehub.arch.town.Regions.dimension();

// A fortified castle: octagonal curtain, round towers with cones, a round keep
Brief castle = Architect.brief("castle", 7, dim, 1.1);
castle.choose("plan", "octagon");
castle.choose("towers", "round");
castle.choose("crown", "cone");
castle.choose("keep", "round");

// A gothic cathedral: twin towers with spires, a transept with a lantern, limestone and slate
Brief cathedral = Architect.brief("cathedral", 2, dim, 1.0).palette("limestone");
cathedral.choose("front", "twin");
cathedral.choose("spires", true);
cathedral.choose("crossing", "lantern");

// A magical tower: five stages under a crystal crown, amethyst palette
Brief tower = Architect.brief("mage_tower", 9, dim, 1.0).palette("amethyst");
tower.choose("stages", 5);
tower.choose("crown", "crystal");

// An ancient ruined complex: a marble tholos, ancient condition
Brief ruin = Architect.brief("temple_ruin", 6, dim, 1.0).palette("marble").condition(Condition.ANCIENT);
ruin.choose("temple", "tholos");

Building b = Architect.build(cathedral, lib, Xform.facing(Dir.SOUTH, 0, 64, 0), null);
Voxels out = new Voxels();
b.emit(out);
Connect.apply(out);
Report report = Validator.building(b);
```

The lobby in `config/battlehub/config.json`:

```json
"lobby": {
  "style": "citadel",
  "seed": 20261017,
  "detail": 2,
  "islandRadius": 150,
  "townDensity": 0.85,
  "discoverModBlocks": false
}
```

## H. Integration

* **Where it runs:** `HubWorld.ensureBuilt` builds the lobby at start-up when the marker (`world/battlehub/hub.json`)
  is missing, is from an older layout version, or was built from another recipe (style, seed, detail, radius,
  density). `/battlehub lobby rebuild` rebuilds it on demand.
* **How blocks are resolved:** the engine produces finished names (`stone_brick_stairs[facing=north,half=top]`).
  `hub/ArchLobby` turns them into block states with `Build.s`. Unknown names come out as air, and the self-test
  reports them. Before generation, `hub/ArchMaterials` checks the catalog against the block registry.
* **How structures are placed:** `ArchLobby` writes the voxels into a `Blueprint`, and `ArenaBuilder` places it
  over several ticks (`arenas.blocksPerTick`). The lobby is generated and validated in about 1.5 s and comes to
  about 440,000 blocks.
* **Adding a module:** implement `Module` (or extend the package's `Base`), draw in roles through `Ctx`, and
  register it in a `Library`. Archetypes ask the library by category and style; `Style.prefer(id, factor)` changes
  how often a style picks it.
* **Adding an archetype:** implement `Archetype` (brief, plan, assemble) and call `Architect.register`.
* **Adding a style or palette:** create a `Style` with tags, proportions and palette recipes. Recipes fill a
  child palette of the district's.
* **Adding materials:** add a line to `Catalog`'s family table (forms, colour, tags, aged variants, stand-in), or
  call `Catalog.add` / `Catalog.discover` at run time.

## I. Known limitations

* **No real game screenshots in CI.** There is no Minecraft client. The ray tracer uses average texture colours,
  not textures. Remedy: a client-side screenshot job with a headless client, if one becomes available.
* **Buildings turn in 90-degree steps.** Diagonal streets and rotated plots would need sub-block rotation or
  re-generation per angle.
* **Interiors are per room.** Upper storeys of houses are empty floors. Remedy: a room planner per storey with a
  circulation graph.
* **Flat terrain only.** The island is a height field, so there are no overhanging cliffs or caves. Underground
  complexes would need a 3D terrain field.
* **Stained glass and colours come from metadata.** The catalog's colours are measured averages, not analysed
  from a resource pack.
* **Spiral stairs use a 3 × 3 core.** Wider monumental stairs inside towers would need another stair module.

## J. Roadmap

1. **Per-storey room planning** with a circulation graph: corridors, rooms behind every window, stairs between
   storeys of houses and halls.
2. **More archetypes on the same modules:** palace (symmetric wings, cour d'honneur, dome), monastery with
   cloister, bridge complexes over ravines, an underground temple (with 3D terrain).
3. **A city wall** round the town, with gatehouses where the avenues cross it (`Fort.curtain` already handles any
   plan).
4. **Diagonal and curved streets** through rotated plot frames, and plazas in front of the landmarks.
5. **Texture-based colour analysis** for discovered mod blocks (when a resource pack is available on the server).
6. **Fingerprints across buildings:** penalise near-identical houses side by side, and keep rare features rare
   across the whole lobby.

## K. Arenas (1.19)

The same engine dresses the arenas where the games are played (`net.battlehub.arch.arena`).

* **Themes (`Dress`).** Each of the 13 arena themes is a dress: one palette for everything in its arenas
  (ground, island rock, paving and the masonry, roofs, timber, glass and lights of its buildings), the style its
  buildings are drawn in, its trees (round, conifer, jungle, cherry, fungus, giant mushroom, palm), ground cover
  and flowers, a scenery feature (boulders, basalt columns, ice spikes, cacti, mesa stacks, obsidian obelisks,
  deepslate spires, prismarine spires, clouds), its lamp, the landmarks that may stand in the distance, its cloth
  colour and an ambient particle. Every theme has two or more variants (another roof, other stone).
* **Bed Wars (`BedWars`, `Kit`, `Isle`).** Every island is a building container in its own frame (local +z
  towards the middle), drawn in roles: the land (`Isle`: a wobbling coastline, a flat top, a tapering underside
  with hanging roots or vines), paving, and the parts of the kit: forge, market stall, lookout, bed gate, ruined
  pavilion, colonnade. Team islands are drawn from one seed, so all of them are the same; the frame is turned to
  the nearest axis towards the middle and mirrored when the middle lies to its left, so with eight teams (an
  octagon turned by 22.5°) every island sees the middle the same way. Diamond islands and the middle are
  four-fold symmetric. Lanes towards the middle, the neighbours and the nearest diamonds (the union over all
  islands, seen from each island's own frame) are kept free of trees and scenery; zones round the spawn, the bed,
  the generator, the shopkeeper and the lookout are carved free after decoration.
* **Surroundings (`Surroundings`, `Backdrop`).** Round a walled arena on the ground: stands (seats on a podium
  wall, a promenade, an outer wall with windows and battlements, gatehouses behind the spawn sides, banners,
  lamps, braziers), and landmarks on rocks beyond, where the spectators look. Round an arena over the void and in
  the open themes: islets and landmarks. Landmarks are archetypes (castle, mage tower, temple ruin, chapel,
  watchtower...) briefed with the dress palette, built once to learn their size, moved out until their rock is
  clear of the play area (for Bed Wars: of the whole square players may build in) and emitted with that offset.
  Nothing is drawn over a column the arena uses.
* **Cover (`Cover`).** Archways, broken colonnades, monuments, wells, crates and stalls, each within a 5 × 5
  footprint. The game places a piece and its copy on the other half with frames turned half round (point
  symmetry) or mirrored (mirror symmetry), from the same seed, so the two are exactly alike.
* **Checks (`ArenaCheck`).** Points present and usable (a bed foot at the bed mark, generators on their blocks,
  room for the shopkeeper, a shop barrel), zones free, walkable from each spawn to its bed, generator and
  shopkeeper, a free coast towards every target within 28° of the straight line that can be walked to, the same
  island for every team (stand/obstacle/open compared point by point in each island's frame), nothing of the
  play area hanging in the air; tactical numbers (cover round the generator, the high ground) for the log.
  JUnit: `ArenaTest`, `SurroundingsTest`. In the game: `ArenaArchTests` (every theme and team count, a map placed
  in the world and walked, duel arenas with stands and symmetric cover, pictures).
* **Integration.** `arena/ArchArenas` turns a plan into a blueprint (marks, spawns, spectator, build box, and the
  "core" box whose chunks stay loaded). `BedWarsLayout` uses it unless `bedwars.json` says `classic`;
  `Scenery.around` uses `Surroundings` unless `arenas.architecture` is off; `Features` adds the cover kinds.
  `fx/Ambience` sends the theme particle round the players.
* **Cost.** A Bed Wars map takes about 10 ms for the play area and 20–120 ms with its landmarks (warm); stands
  with landmarks about 30–80 ms. Arenas come to 20,000–100,000 blocks.
* **Limits.** Islands still turn in 90° steps: with 3, 5, 6 or 7 teams the islands face the middle less exactly
  than with 2, 4 or 8 (as before), although every island is still the same. The stands are round, so square
  arenas get a paved court between their walls and the seats.
